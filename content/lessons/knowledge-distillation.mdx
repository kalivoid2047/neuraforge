<h2 id="intuition">Closing week three: compressing a large model into a small one</h2>

Alignment produces a capable model that may be far too large to serve. **Distillation** transfers its behaviour into a smaller **student** by training the student to match the **teacher's full output distribution** rather than just the correct answer.

<h2 id="math">Learning From a Distribution, Not a Label</h2>

The student minimizes a KL divergence against the teacher's temperature-softened distribution:

<MathBlock tex="\mathcal{L} = \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(y, \hat{y}) + (1-\alpha)\,T^2 \cdot D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(p_{\text{teacher}}^{T} \,\|\, p_{\text{student}}^{T}\right)" />

Both distributions use temperature `T` from Month 8. The `T²` factor compensates for the fact that softening shrinks the gradients by roughly `1/T²`, keeping the two loss terms comparable in scale as `T` varies.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Dark knowledge: the wrong answers carry the information">
  A hard label says an image is a `7`. A teacher's distribution says it is a `7` with 0.9, a `1` with 0.08, and a `9` with 0.015 — encoding that sevens resemble ones far more than they resemble fours. Hinton called this **dark knowledge**: the *relative* probabilities among wrong answers describe a similarity structure the hard label discards entirely. Raising `T` amplifies those small probabilities so the student can actually learn from them, which is exactly why distillation uses `T > 1` while inference uses `T ≈ 1`.
</Callout>

## Summary

Distillation trains a small student to match a large teacher's full output distribution rather than hard labels, because the relative probabilities among wrong answers encode a similarity structure — dark knowledge — that a one-hot label discards. Temperature `T > 1` amplifies those small probabilities so they contribute usable gradient, with a `T²` factor keeping the loss terms balanced. Variants differ in what is matched: output distributions (response), internal representations (feature), or judgements between candidates (preference, which reuses the Bradley-Terry machinery from this week).
