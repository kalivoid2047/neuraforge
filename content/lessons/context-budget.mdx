<h2 id="intuition">A bigger context window is not permission to use it</h2>

Context windows grew from 2k to 128k tokens and beyond, and the obvious move is to retrieve more. That instinct is usually wrong on two counts: it costs real memory, and it tends to make answers worse rather than better.

<h2 id="math">What Fits, and What It Costs</h2>

The budget is what remains after reserving room for the answer and the instructions:

<MathBlock tex="\text{usable} = \text{window} - \text{max\_output} - \text{instruction overhead}" />

Divide by chunk size for the number of chunks. But the cost side comes straight from Month 8: the KV cache grows linearly with sequence length, reaching **85.90 GB** at 32k context on an 80-layer model. Every retrieved chunk is paid for in memory on every request.

<Callout kind="warning" title="More context frequently degrades answers">
  Retrieval quality falls off after the top few results, so chunks 20 through 200 are mostly distractors. Models also attend unevenly across long contexts — information in the middle is used less reliably than information at either end. The practical consequence: retrieving 5 well-ranked chunks usually beats retrieving 50, and it is cheaper. Fill the budget only when measurement says it helps.
</Callout>

## Summary

Usable context is the window minus reserved output and instructions, giving roughly 28 chunks at 4k and 1,030 at 128k. But capacity is the wrong thing to optimize: retrieval quality falls off quickly, models use mid-context material less reliably, and Month 8 measured the KV cache reaching 85.90 GB at 32k on an 80-layer model. Five well-ranked chunks usually beat fifty. The next lesson improves the ranking rather than expanding the budget.
