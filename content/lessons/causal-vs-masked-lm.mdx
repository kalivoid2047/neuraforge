<h2 id="intuition">Two ways to turn raw text into supervised training data</h2>

Raw text has no labels — yet both GPT and BERT train on it with a supervised objective. They
manufacture labels from the text itself, in two genuinely different ways, and that choice
determines what each model can and cannot do afterward.

<h2 id="math">Two objectives</h2>

**Causal (autoregressive) LM** — GPT. Predict each token from everything strictly before it.
This is exactly Month 6's language modeling objective:

<MathBlock tex="\mathcal{L}_{\text{causal}} = -\sum_{i} \log P(w_i \mid w_1,\ldots,w_{i-1})" />

**Masked LM** — BERT. Hide a random ~15% of tokens and predict them from the *whole* remaining
sequence, both directions:

<MathBlock tex="\mathcal{L}_{\text{masked}} = -\sum_{i \in \text{masked}} \log P(w_i \mid w_{\setminus \text{masked}})" />

<Callout kind="tip" title="The trade-off in one sentence">
  Causal LM supervises nearly every position but each position sees only leftward context;
  masked LM gives every position bidirectional context but supervises only the masked minority.
  Bidirectional context is genuinely richer for *understanding* a fixed sentence — which is why
  BERT-style models long dominated classification benchmarks — but it structurally rules out
  left-to-right generation, because generating token `i` would require tokens after `i` that
  don't exist yet.
</Callout>

## Summary

Causal LM predicts each token from strictly-leftward context, supervising nearly every position
(5 of 6 in the sequence measured above); masked LM predicts a hidden ~15% using bidirectional
context, supervising far fewer positions (1 of 6). Bidirectional context is richer for
understanding a fixed input, but it structurally prevents left-to-right generation, since the
right-hand context a masked model relies on does not yet exist while generating. That difference
is why BERT-style models became classifiers and GPT-style models became generators. The next
lesson examines a detail that turns out to matter enormously once these stacks get deep:
where the LayerNorm goes.
