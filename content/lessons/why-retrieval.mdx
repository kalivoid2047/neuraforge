<h2 id="intuition">Month 10: giving a model access to things it was never trained on</h2>

Months 8 and 9 put knowledge **into weights** — first by pretraining, then by fine-tuning. That knowledge is fixed at training time, expensive to update, and impossible to cite. **Retrieval** puts knowledge somewhere else entirely: an external store the model queries at inference. Facts can then change without retraining, and every claim can point at a source.

<h2 id="math">Two Places Knowledge Can Live</h2>

**Parametric** knowledge lives in `θ`. Updating it means training; inspecting it is hard; attributing it is impossible.

**Non-parametric** knowledge lives in a document store. The model receives it as context:

<MathBlock tex="P(y \mid x) \;\longrightarrow\; P\big(y \mid x,\; \text{retrieve}(x)\big)" />

Nothing about the model changes — only what it is shown. That single substitution is the whole of RAG.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Retrieval and fine-tuning solve different problems">
  A rule of thumb that survives contact with practice: **fine-tune to change behaviour, retrieve to change knowledge.** If the model answers in the wrong format or register, that is a fine-tuning problem. If it does not know your company's Q3 numbers, no amount of fine-tuning is the right fix — the facts change monthly and you would retrain forever. Month 10's final week returns to this comparison with measurements.
</Callout>

## Summary

Knowledge can live in weights (parametric) or in an external store (non-parametric). Retrieval swaps `P(y|x)` for `P(y|x, retrieve(x))` without changing the model at all — so facts update instantly and answers can cite sources. Fine-tune to change behaviour; retrieve to change knowledge. The vector space built above over 18 real curriculum lessons is the foundation for the rest of the month.
