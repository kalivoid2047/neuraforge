<h2 id="intuition">Closing week two: the combination that made 7B fine-tuning ordinary</h2>

LoRA removed the optimizer state; quantization removes most of the weight storage. **QLoRA** is exactly their composition: freeze the base model in **NF4**, attach **bf16** LoRA adapters, and train only the adapters. The result fits a 7B fine-tune into single-digit gigabytes.

<h2 id="math">Two Precisions in One Model</h2>

The two precisions serve different roles:

- The **base** is frozen and only ever read in a forward pass, so 4-bit is sufficient.
- The **adapters** receive gradients and Adam updates, where 4-bit would be far too coarse — the quantization error measured last lesson (19.50% at 4 bits) would swamp the updates entirely.

<MathBlock tex="h = x\,\text{dequant}(W_0^{\text{NF4}}) + \frac{\alpha}{r}(xA^\top)B^\top \quad\text{with }A,B\text{ in bf16}" />

Weights are dequantized to compute-precision on the fly, block by block, so the 4-bit form is a *storage* format rather than an arithmetic one.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Gradients flow through the quantized base — they just don't stop there">
  A common confusion: if the base is quantized and frozen, how does anything learn? The backward pass still propagates gradient *through* the base weights to reach the adapters — the chain rule needs `W₀` to compute `∂L/∂x`, and it uses the dequantized values for that. What does not happen is any gradient being **stored for or applied to** `W₀`. Frozen means no update, not no participation.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="tip" title="What QLoRA costs you">
  Nothing is free. Dequantizing on every forward pass costs **time** — QLoRA typically trains
  meaningfully slower than bf16 LoRA on the same hardware. And the base model's 4-bit
  approximation introduces a small quality floor that no amount of adapter training removes.
  The trade is straightforward: QLoRA is what you use when the model would not otherwise fit,
  and bf16 LoRA is what you use when it would.
</Callout>

## Summary

QLoRA freezes the base in NF4 and trains bf16 LoRA adapters on top, composing week two's two savings. Measured for a 7B model: 112 GB full fine-tuning, 14.1 GB LoRA on a bf16 base, and **3.6 GB** with an NF4 base — counting weights and optimizer state, with activations on top. Gradients still flow through the dequantized base to reach the adapters; frozen means unchanged, not uninvolved. The costs are slower training from on-the-fly dequantization and a small quality floor from the 4-bit approximation. Week three turns from efficiency to what you actually train on.
