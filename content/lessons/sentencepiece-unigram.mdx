<h2 id="intuition">Two more ideas: no whitespace assumption, and segmentation as inference</h2>

**SentencePiece** contributes two distinct ideas on top of BPE/WordPiece. First: it treats the
input as a raw stream of characters — including spaces, encoded as an ordinary symbol (often
written `▁`) — rather than assuming text is already pre-split on whitespace. That single choice
makes tokenization work identically for English (which uses spaces) and Chinese or Japanese
(which don't), with no language-specific pre-processing rules. Second: SentencePiece's
**Unigram LM** mode reframes tokenization itself as a maximum likelihood problem (Month 5, week
two) — given a vocabulary of subword pieces each with a probability, find the segmentation of a
word that *maximizes the product of its pieces' probabilities*.

<h2 id="math">Segmentation as maximum likelihood</h2>

Given a vocabulary of pieces with unigram probabilities `P(\text{piece})`, a word can usually be
split multiple different ways. The Unigram LM picks the segmentation `s_1, \ldots, s_k` that
maximizes the sequence probability:

<MathBlock tex="\hat{s} = \arg\max_{s_1,\ldots,s_k} \prod_{i=1}^{k} P(s_i)" />

This is solved efficiently with **Viterbi-style dynamic programming**: build up the best
(highest-probability) segmentation of each prefix of the word from the best segmentations of its
shorter prefixes, rather than enumerating every possible way to split the word (which grows
exponentially with word length).

<Callout kind="tip" title="Bottom-up merging vs. top-down probabilistic modeling">
  BPE and WordPiece both *build* a vocabulary bottom-up, one merge at a time, and tokenize
  greedily by replaying those merges. Unigram LM instead starts from a large candidate vocabulary,
  assigns every piece a probability, and *tokenizes* by finding the maximum-likelihood
  segmentation under that probability model — training then iteratively prunes the vocabulary
  down, keeping whichever pieces matter most for the overall likelihood. It's a genuinely
  different framing: segmentation as inference under a probabilistic model, not as replaying a
  fixed merge sequence.
</Callout>

<h2 id="python">Viterbi segmentation, verified against brute-force enumeration</h2>

<CodeCell
  code={"from itertools import product\n\n# a small Unigram LM vocabulary: piece -> probability (would be learned from a corpus in practice)\nvocab_probs = {\n    \"low\": 0.20, \"est\": 0.15, \"er\": 0.05, \"l\": 0.02, \"o\": 0.03, \"w\": 0.02,\n    \"e\": 0.04, \"s\": 0.03, \"t\": 0.03, \"r\": 0.02, \"lo\": 0.06, \"we\": 0.01,\n}\n\ndef all_segmentations(word):\n    \"\"\"Brute force: every way to split 'word' into a sequence of vocabulary substrings.\"\"\"\n    if word == \"\":\n        yield []\n        return\n    for i in range(1, len(word) + 1):\n        prefix = word[:i]\n        if prefix in vocab_probs:\n            for rest in all_segmentations(word[i:]):\n                yield [prefix] + rest\n\ndef segmentation_probability(segments):\n    prob = 1.0\n    for s in segments:\n        prob *= vocab_probs[s]\n    return prob\n\nword = \"lower\"\nall_splits = list(all_segmentations(word))\nprint(f\"all valid segmentations of '{word}' using only in-vocabulary pieces:\")\nfor seg in all_splits:\n    print(f\"  {seg}  ->  probability = {segmentation_probability(seg):.8f}\")\n\nbrute_force_best = max(all_splits, key=segmentation_probability)\nprint()\nprint(f\"brute-force best segmentation: {brute_force_best}\")\nprint()\n\n# now solve the SAME problem with Viterbi dynamic programming -- no exponential enumeration\ndef viterbi_segment(word, vocab_probs):\n    n = len(word)\n    best_prob = [0.0] * (n + 1)\n    best_prob[0] = 1.0\n    backpointer = [None] * (n + 1)\n    for i in range(1, n + 1):\n        for j in range(i):\n            piece = word[j:i]\n            if piece in vocab_probs and best_prob[j] > 0:\n                candidate = best_prob[j] * vocab_probs[piece]\n                if candidate > best_prob[i]:\n                    best_prob[i] = candidate\n                    backpointer[i] = j\n    # reconstruct the segmentation by following backpointers from the end\n    segments = []\n    i = n\n    while i > 0:\n        j = backpointer[i]\n        segments.append(word[j:i])\n        i = j\n    return list(reversed(segments)), best_prob[n]\n\nviterbi_result, viterbi_prob = viterbi_segment(word, vocab_probs)\nprint(f\"Viterbi DP result: {viterbi_result}  ->  probability = {viterbi_prob:.8f}\")\nprint(f\"matches brute-force best: {viterbi_result == brute_force_best}\")"}

## Summary

SentencePiece treats whitespace as an ordinary symbol, making tokenization language-agnostic,
and its Unigram LM mode reframes tokenization as maximum likelihood segmentation — finding the
piece sequence maximizing the product of vocabulary probabilities, computed efficiently via
Viterbi dynamic programming rather than exponential brute-force enumeration, verified directly
above to produce identical results. This is a genuinely different framing from BPE/WordPiece's
bottom-up greedy merging: segmentation as probabilistic inference. The next, final lesson this
week compares all three tokenizers directly on the properties that matter in practice.
