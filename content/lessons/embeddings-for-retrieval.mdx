<h2 id="intuition">Month 6 embedded words; retrieval needs to embed whole passages</h2>

Month 6 learned a vector per **word**. Retrieval needs a vector per **document**, such that a query vector lands near the documents that answer it. The cell below builds exactly that with TF-IDF — a lexical vector space that is fully transparent and needs no training — and the rest of the week measures precisely where it falls down.

<h2 id="math">From Word Vectors to Document Vectors</h2>

Each document becomes a vector over the vocabulary, weighted by TF-IDF (Month 5):

<MathBlock tex="x_{d,w} = \text{tf}(w,d)\cdot \text{idf}(w) \qquad \text{idf}(w)=\log\frac{N+1}{\text{df}(w)+1}+1" />

A query is embedded the same way and compared by cosine similarity. The critical property is that **query and document live in the same space**, so a single dot product ranks everything.

<Callout kind="warning" title="A lexical space can only match words that literally appear">
  TF-IDF has no notion that *arrow with direction* and *vector* are related — they share no term, so their similarity is zero. Learned **bi-encoders** (a Transformer encoding query and document into a shared space, trained on relevance pairs) fix this by placing paraphrases near each other. Everything structural in this month applies to both; only the embedding function changes. Week four measures exactly how much this limitation costs.
</Callout>

## Summary

Retrieval embeds whole documents rather than individual words, placing queries and documents in one space so a dot product ranks them. TF-IDF does this transparently and without training, and is genuinely useful — but it matches on shared **terms**, so a paraphrase with no overlapping vocabulary scores zero. Learned bi-encoders close that gap while leaving every other part of the pipeline unchanged.
