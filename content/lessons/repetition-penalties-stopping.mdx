<h2 id="intuition">Two remaining generation failures</h2>

Truncated sampling fixes incoherence, but two problems survive. First, models still fall into
**repetition loops** — once a phrase appears, conditioning on it makes the phrase more likely
again, and the model can spiral. Second, nothing tells generation to **stop**; a language model
predicts a next token indefinitely, so termination must be imposed from outside.

<h2 id="math">Two penalty formulations</h2>

**Repetition penalty** (CTRL-style) divides the logit of any already-generated token — flat,
regardless of how many times it appeared:

<MathBlock tex="z'_w = \begin{cases} z_w / \rho & \text{if } w \text{ appeared and } z_w > 0 \\ z_w \cdot \rho & \text{if } w \text{ appeared and } z_w \leq 0 \end{cases}" />

The sign split exists because dividing a negative logit would *increase* it, rewarding the
repeat rather than punishing it.

**Frequency penalty** subtracts an amount proportional to the token's **count**:

<MathBlock tex="z'_w = z_w - \alpha \cdot \text{count}(w)" />

<Callout kind="tip" title="Presence vs frequency">
  A third variant, **presence penalty**, subtracts a flat constant if the token appeared at all
  (count ≥ 1), regardless of how often. The three form a natural progression: presence penalizes
  *appearing*, frequency penalizes *how often*, and repetition rescales multiplicatively. APIs
  commonly expose presence and frequency as separate parameters precisely because they encourage
  different things — topic variety versus vocabulary variety.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning" title="Penalties suppress legitimate repetition too">
  These operate on raw token identity with no notion of whether a repeat is appropriate. Function
  words (`the`, `of`, `is`) *should* recur constantly, and so should a subject's name in a
  paragraph about it. Notice above that at `α = 1.0` the frequency penalty drove `P(the)` down to
  `0.0122` — a nearly forty-fold suppression of an ordinary English article. Aggressive penalty
  settings are a common cause of oddly stilted output that avoids necessary words.
</Callout>

## Summary

Repetition penalty divides the logits of already-seen tokens by a flat factor; frequency penalty
subtracts an amount proportional to how many times each appeared. That difference is decisive:
with `the` used four times and `cat` once, `α = 0.3` frequency penalty promoted `cat` above `the`
while repetition penalty at 1.1 preserved the original ranking. Both risk suppressing legitimate
repetition — at `α = 1.0`, `P(the)` collapsed to 0.0122. Stopping requires an EOS token, a token
cap, or stop strings, since nothing intrinsic ends generation. The next lesson makes generation
fast.
