<h2 id="intuition">Three operations, and one of them is doing quiet double duty</h2>

Vector addition and scalar multiplication are exactly what they sound like: add corresponding
components, or scale every component by the same number. The **dot product** is the one worth
slowing down for — it looks like simple arithmetic but secretly measures *how aligned* two
vectors are, and that single fact is the computational seed of attention (Month 7), of
similarity search, and of most of what "AI understands meaning" actually reduces to
mechanically.

<h2 id="math">The dot product, two equivalent ways</h2>

**Algebraically** — multiply corresponding components, sum the results:

<MathBlock tex="\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i b_i" />

**Geometrically** — the same number equals the product of both lengths times the cosine of the
angle between them:

<MathBlock tex="\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = |\vec{a}|\,|\vec{b}|\cos\theta" />

These are the *same value*, computed two different ways — proving that isn't this lesson's job
(it's a standard exercise in any linear algebra text), but the consequence is what matters:
since `|a|` and `|b|` are always positive, the *sign* of the dot product tells you about the
angle directly. Positive → angle less than 90°. Zero → exactly 90° (orthogonal). Negative →
angle greater than 90°.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Why this matters for AI, concretely">
  "How similar are these two word embeddings?" is, mechanically, almost always "compute their
  dot product (or a close relative of it, cosine similarity)." Two words with similar meaning
  end up as vectors pointing in similar directions, learned automatically from data — Month 6
  builds exactly this.
</Callout>

## Summary

Vector addition and scalar multiplication work component-wise. The dot product
(`sum(a_i * b_i)`) equals `|a||b|cos(θ)` — the same value computed two ways — which means its
*sign* directly reveals whether the angle between two vectors is acute (positive), exactly
90° (zero, orthogonal), or obtuse (negative). This is the computational basis for measuring
similarity between vectors throughout AI.
