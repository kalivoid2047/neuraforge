<h2 id="intuition">Closing week two: the general principle behind cross-entropy training</h2>

Every parameter estimation this month — and every neural network weight ever trained with
cross-entropy loss — is secretly an instance of one general statistical principle: **maximum
likelihood estimation (MLE)**. Given observed data and a family of candidate models (parameter
values), choose the parameters that make the *observed data* as probable as possible under the
model. This lesson makes that principle precise, and shows — not just claims — that training a
classifier with cross-entropy loss literally *is* maximum likelihood estimation.

<h2 id="math">The MLE principle</h2>

For data `x_1, \ldots, x_n` (assumed independent) and a model with parameter `θ`, the
**likelihood** of the data is the probability the model assigns to it:

<MathBlock tex="L(\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} P(x_i \mid \theta) \qquad \hat\theta_{\text{MLE}} = \arg\max_\theta L(\theta)" />

Products of many small probabilities underflow numerically, so in practice you maximize the
**log-likelihood** instead — the logarithm turns the product into a sum, and since `log` is
monotonically increasing, it never changes *which* `θ` is the maximizer:

<MathBlock tex="\log L(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log P(x_i \mid \theta)" />

For `n` independent Bernoulli(`p`) trials with `k` observed successes, maximizing the
log-likelihood with respect to `p` (via ordinary calculus — setting the derivative to zero, a
direct application of Month 3's tools) gives a strikingly simple closed form:

<MathBlock tex="\hat{p}_{\text{MLE}} = \frac{k}{n} \qquad \text{(just the observed success rate)}" />

<Callout kind="tip" title="Cross-entropy minimization IS maximum likelihood estimation">
  For a classification dataset, the **negative** log-likelihood of the correct labels under the
  model, summed (or averaged) over all examples, is *exactly* the total cross-entropy loss
  Month 4 minimized: `-\sum_i \log Q(y_i | x_i)`. Minimizing cross-entropy loss and maximizing
  the likelihood of the true labels are the same optimization problem, just with a sign flip and
  a name. Every classifier trained this way is doing maximum likelihood estimation, whether or
  not that name was ever mentioned.
</Callout>

<h2 id="python">The Bernoulli MLE, verified against a numerical search</h2>

<CodeCell
  code={"import numpy as np\n\nrng = np.random.default_rng(0)\n\n# generate data from a coin with a KNOWN true bias, then pretend we don't know it\ntrue_p = 0.7\nn_flips = 200\nflips = (rng.random(n_flips) < true_p).astype(int)   # 1 = heads, 0 = tails\nk = flips.sum()\nprint(f\"observed: {k} heads out of {n_flips} flips\")\n\n# closed-form MLE: just the observed success rate\np_mle_closed_form = k / n_flips\nprint(f\"closed-form MLE: p_hat = k/n = {p_mle_closed_form:.4f}\")\nprint()\n\n# verify by brute-force: compute the log-likelihood for many candidate p values, find the max\ndef log_likelihood(p, flips):\n    # avoid log(0) at the p=0 or p=1 boundary\n    p = np.clip(p, 1e-10, 1 - 1e-10)\n    return np.sum(flips * np.log(p) + (1 - flips) * np.log(1 - p))\n\ncandidate_ps = np.linspace(0.01, 0.99, 9801)   # a fine grid of candidate p values\nlog_likelihoods = [log_likelihood(p, flips) for p in candidate_ps]\np_mle_grid_search = candidate_ps[np.argmax(log_likelihoods)]\nprint(f\"grid-search MLE (best of {len(candidate_ps)} candidates): p_hat = {p_mle_grid_search:.4f}\")\nprint(f\"matches closed-form: {np.isclose(p_mle_closed_form, p_mle_grid_search, atol=1e-3)}\")\nprint()\n\n# also confirm: NEGATIVE log-likelihood at the MLE equals the average cross-entropy loss\navg_neg_log_likelihood = -log_likelihood(p_mle_closed_form, flips) / n_flips\ncross_entropy_style = -np.mean(flips * np.log(p_mle_closed_form) + (1 - flips) * np.log(1 - p_mle_closed_form))\nprint(f\"average negative log-likelihood at p_hat: {avg_neg_log_likelihood:.4f}\")\nprint(f\"(this IS the binary cross-entropy loss, evaluated at the MLE)\")\nprint(f\"identical to direct cross-entropy computation: {np.isclose(avg_neg_log_likelihood, cross_entropy_style)}\")"}
  output={"observed: 127 heads out of 200 flips\nclosed-form MLE: p_hat = k/n = 0.6350\n\ngrid-search MLE (best of 9801 candidates): p_hat = 0.6350\nmatches closed-form: True\n\naverage negative log-likelihood at p_hat: 0.6562\n(this IS the binary cross-entropy loss, evaluated at the MLE)\nidentical to direct cross-entropy computation: True"}

## Week two summary

This week grounded Month 4's cross-entropy loss in information theory and statistics: entropy
measures a distribution's own uncertainty; cross-entropy measures the cost of using a
mismatched distribution to encode another's outcomes; KL divergence isolates that mismatch cost
exactly; softmax is the unique, non-arbitrary way to turn logits into probabilities; and
training with cross-entropy loss is precisely maximum likelihood estimation, verified above via
the classic Bernoulli case. None of this changes how any Month 4 code was written — it explains
*why* that code was the right choice. Week three turns to representing text itself as data,
building toward this month's Text Classifier capstone.
