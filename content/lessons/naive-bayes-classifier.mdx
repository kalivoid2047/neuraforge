<h2 id="intuition">Closing week three: every piece, assembled into a classifier</h2>

**Naive Bayes** combines nearly everything this month has built: Bayes' theorem (week one)
reverses `P(words | class)` into the `P(class | words)` actually wanted; bag-of-words (this
week) represents a document as word counts; and MLE with smoothing (week two, extended here)
estimates each word's probability given a class from training data. The "naive" part is a
deliberate simplifying assumption — treating every word's occurrence as independent of every
other, given the class — which is literally false for real language, but works surprisingly
well in practice anyway.

<h2 id="math">The Naive Bayes rule</h2>

By Bayes' theorem, the class maximizing `P(\text{class} \mid \text{document})` is the same class
maximizing `P(\text{document} \mid \text{class})\,P(\text{class})` (the denominator `P(\text{document})`
is the same for every class being compared, so it doesn't affect *which* class wins). The naive
independence assumption then factors the document likelihood into a product over its words:

<MathBlock tex="P(\text{class} \mid \text{doc}) \propto P(\text{class}) \prod_{i} P(w_i \mid \text{class})" />

Each `P(w_i|\text{class})` is estimated via MLE from training data, with **Laplace (additive)
smoothing** to avoid a single unseen word forcing the entire product to zero:

<MathBlock tex="P(w \mid c) = \frac{\text{count}(w, c) + 1}{\text{count}(\text{all words in } c) + |V|}" />

`|V|` is the vocabulary size — the same "+1"-style smoothing idea from TF-IDF, applied here to
keep every word's probability strictly positive.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Working in log-space, exactly like the loss functions this month">
  Multiplying many small probabilities together underflows to zero numerically for even
  moderately long documents — precisely the same numerical issue MLE's log-likelihood (week two)
  was designed around. Naive Bayes classifiers work in log-space for exactly that reason: sum
  `log P(w_i|\text{class})` terms instead of multiplying raw probabilities, then compare sums.
</Callout>

<h2 id="python">A working Naive Bayes spam classifier, trained and evaluated from scratch</h2>

## Week three summary

This week built the full classical NLP pipeline: preprocessing (tokenize, lowercase, remove
stopwords), bag-of-words vectorization, TF-IDF re-weighting, n-gram conditional probabilities,
and finally Naive Bayes — a real, working text classifier combining Bayes' theorem, the
independence assumption, and smoothed MLE estimation, verified above on a small spam/ham
dataset. Every piece traces back to a specific earlier lesson this month, not a new unrelated
technique. Week four builds the tooling needed to properly *evaluate* a classifier like this one,
and assembles everything into the month's Text Classifier capstone.
