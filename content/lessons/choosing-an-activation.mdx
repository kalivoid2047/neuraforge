<h2 id="intuition">Closing week three: putting it all together</h2>

This week covered sigmoid, tanh, ReLU, Leaky ReLU, GELU, and softmax individually. In practice,
a single network typically uses *different* activations in different places — a specific choice
for hidden layers, a different, task-dependent choice for the output layer. This closing lesson
is about making that choice deliberately rather than by habit.

<h2 id="checklist">A practical decision checklist</h2>

<Callout kind="tip" title="Hidden layers">
  Default to **ReLU** (fast, simple, works well in most cases) or **GELU** (smoother, standard
  in modern Transformers, slightly more expensive). Reach for **Leaky ReLU** specifically if
  you're observing dying-neuron symptoms (many activations stuck at exactly zero). Sigmoid and
  tanh are rarely the right choice for hidden layers today, given their saturation issues — you
  will mostly see them explained for historical/conceptual reasons, as this week did.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="tip" title="Output layers, by task">
  **Binary classification** (2 classes): a single **sigmoid** output, interpreted as
  `P(class = 1)`. **Multi-class classification** (mutually exclusive classes): **softmax** over
  one output per class. **Regression** (predicting a continuous number): typically **no
  activation at all** (a "linear" output layer) — you generally don't want to artificially
  bound a regression target's range unless the target itself is naturally bounded.
</Callout>

## Summary

Activation choice depends on role: ReLU/GELU for hidden layers by default (Leaky ReLU if
dying-neuron symptoms appear), sigmoid for binary-classification output, softmax for
multi-class-classification output, and typically no activation at all for regression output.
This closes out the activation-function toolkit; week four assembles everything from this month
— perceptrons, activations, and Month 1's matrix operations — into a full forward pass and this
month's Neural Network From Scratch capstone.
