<h2 id="intuition">A single knob between deterministic and chaotic</h2>

Greedy is one extreme (always the mode), pure sampling another (faithful to the full
distribution including its tail). **Temperature** is the continuous dial between them and
beyond — one number that reshapes how peaked or flat the distribution is before you sample from
it.

<h2 id="math">One divisor</h2>

Divide the logits by `T` before the softmax:

<MathBlock tex="P_T(w) = \frac{\exp(z_w / T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j / T)}" />

- `T = 1` leaves the model's own distribution untouched.
- `T < 1` **sharpens** it — differences between logits are magnified, probability concentrates on
  the top tokens. As `T → 0` this converges to greedy.
- `T > 1` **flattens** it — differences shrink, probability spreads toward the tail. As `T → ∞`
  it converges to uniform.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Temperature reshapes, it never reorders">
  Dividing every logit by the same positive `T` is a monotonic transformation, so the *ranking*
  of tokens is preserved exactly. The most likely token at `T=0.1` is the most likely token at
  `T=5.0`. What changes is only how much probability mass sits on it — which is why temperature
  alone cannot make a model prefer a different word, only make it more or less willing to
  consider alternatives.
</Callout>

## Summary

Temperature divides logits by `T` before the softmax, sharpening the distribution when `T < 1`
(entropy fell to 0.13 bits at `T=0.1`, with the top token at 0.9820 — effectively greedy) and
flattening it when `T > 1` (2.90 bits at `T=3.0`, near the 3.00-bit uniform ceiling). It is
strictly monotonic, so token ranking is preserved exactly at every temperature. That is also its
limitation: it rescales the entire distribution and cannot selectively remove the incoherent
tail. The next lesson truncates that tail directly.
