<h2 id="intuition">Cut the tail off before sampling from it</h2>

Temperature reshapes the whole distribution but cannot remove the incoherent tail — flattening
makes the tail *more* likely, and sharpening suppresses it only by collapsing toward greedy.
**Truncation** solves this directly: discard the unlikely tokens outright, renormalize what
remains, and sample from that. Two rules dominate, and the difference between them is whether
the cutoff can adapt.

<h2 id="math">Two truncation rules</h2>

**Top-k**: keep the `k` highest-probability tokens, discard everything else, renormalize.

<MathBlock tex="V_k = \text{top-}k\text{ tokens by } P(w) \qquad P'(w) = \frac{P(w)}{\sum_{v \in V_k} P(v)} \text{ for } w \in V_k" />

**Top-p (nucleus)**: keep the *smallest* set of tokens whose cumulative probability reaches `p`.

<MathBlock tex="V_p = \text{smallest set with} \sum_{v \in V_p} P(v) \geq p \qquad P'(w) = \frac{P(w)}{\sum_{v \in V_p} P(v)}" />

The distinction is that `k` is a fixed *count* while `p` is a mass target — so top-p keeps
however many tokens that particular distribution requires, which varies from step to step.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Why adaptivity matters more than it sounds">
  Consider two positions in one generation. After "the capital of France is" the model is nearly
  certain, and any `k > 1` forces you to keep plausible-but-wrong alternatives. After "he walked
  into the" the model is genuinely uncertain across many valid nouns, and `k = 3` discards most
  of the legitimate variety. A single fixed `k` is simultaneously too loose in the first case and
  too tight in the second. Top-p handles both correctly with one parameter.
</Callout>

## Summary

Top-k keeps a fixed count of tokens; top-p (nucleus) keeps the smallest set reaching a target
probability mass. That distinction makes top-p adaptive: with `p=0.9` it kept 1 token where the
model was confident and 7 where it was uncertain, while `k=3` would have been simultaneously too
permissive in the first case and too restrictive in the second. Temperature and top-p compose
naturally — sharpening the distribution first means fewer tokens are needed to reach the mass
target (2 tokens at `T=0.3` rising to 7 at `T=2.0`), which is why production APIs expose both.
