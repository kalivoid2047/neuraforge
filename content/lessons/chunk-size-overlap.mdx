<h2 id="intuition">Overlap is insurance you pay for in storage</h2>

Chunk size controls how specific each vector is; overlap controls how likely a statement is to survive intact somewhere. Both are measurable, and the storage cost of overlap is exactly computable — the table below does it.

<h2 id="math">Two Dials, One Redundancy Cost</h2>

With chunk size `s` and overlap `v`, consecutive chunks advance by `s - v` tokens, so for a document of `n` tokens:

<MathBlock tex="\#\text{chunks} \approx \left\lceil\frac{n}{s-v}\right\rceil \qquad \text{tokens stored} \approx n\cdot\frac{s}{s-v}" />

At 50% overlap the multiplier is `2×`: you index the whole corpus twice. That is the price of boundary insurance.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Choosing a size">
  Small chunks (~100 tokens) give precise matching but often lack the surrounding context needed to be understood on their own. Large chunks (~1000 tokens) are self-contained but dilute the vector and burn context budget. A common starting point is 200–500 tokens with 10–20% overlap, then tune against a real evaluation set — which week four builds. Chunk size is an empirical parameter, not a principled constant.
</Callout>

## Summary

Chunk size sets specificity; overlap sets boundary safety. The cost is exactly `s/(s-v)` — measured at `1.88×` for size 20 with overlap 10, and roughly `2×` at 50% overlap. That multiplier applies to vectors, storage, and per-query comparisons alike. Typical starting points are 200–500 tokens with 10–20% overlap, tuned against a real evaluation set rather than chosen on principle.
