<h2 id="intuition">Week four: proving the fine-tune actually worked</h2>

Week one showed fine-tuning can destroy capabilities nobody was measuring. That makes evaluation design the difference between a fine-tune that works and one that merely *appears* to. A credible report covers three axes: did the target improve, did everything else survive, and did safety behaviour hold.

<h2 id="math">Three Axes, Not One</h2>

The three axes and what each catches:

- **Target** — the capability you fine-tuned for. Necessary, and on its own worthless as evidence.
- **Retained** — capabilities you did *not* fine-tune on. Catches catastrophic forgetting.
- **Safety** — refusal behaviour on harmful requests. Catches alignment erosion, which narrow fine-tuning degrades even when nobody targets it.

And one precondition over all three: the benchmark must not appear in the training data.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Contamination invalidates every number above it">
  If benchmark items leak into the fine-tuning set, the model memorizes rather than generalizes and the reported score measures recall. The scenario below shows an 18% leak inflating accuracy by 16 points. Decontaminate before training: hash benchmark items, check for exact and near-duplicate matches, and remove them. Doing it afterwards is not possible — the model has already seen them.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning" title="These are constructed scenarios, not measurements">
  Unlike most cells in this curriculum, the before/after scores below are **illustrative inputs**
  chosen to show a realistic pattern — they were not produced by running a real fine-tune. The
  *arithmetic* on them is genuine (the deltas, the regression count, the contamination inflation),
  but the underlying numbers are stipulated.

  The capstone at the end of this week uses **real measured** numbers from an actual training run.
  Treat this lesson as a template for the report you should produce, and the capstone as the

## Summary

A fine-tune evaluation covers three axes: target capability, retained capability, and safety. In the scenario above the target improved `+0.47` — which alone reads as a clear success — while three capabilities regressed, including a `-0.33` collapse in refusal behaviour that nobody fine-tuned against. Contamination is a precondition rather than an axis: an 18% benchmark leak inflated reported accuracy by 16 points through memorization alone. The next lesson examines the capability-versus-alignment trade these reports keep surfacing.
