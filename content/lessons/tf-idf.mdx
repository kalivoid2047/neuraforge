<h2 id="intuition">Not every word is equally informative</h2>

Plain bag-of-words counts have a blind spot: a word appearing in *every* document (like "the")
gets a large count in every document's vector, contributing nothing useful for telling documents
apart, while a word appearing in only one or two documents (like a topic-specific term) is far
more informative for distinguishing them — yet plain counts don't reflect that difference at
all. **TF-IDF** fixes this by explicitly down-weighting words that are common *across the whole
corpus*, not just within one document.

<h2 id="math">Term frequency and inverse document frequency</h2>

<MathBlock tex="\text{TF}(t,d) = \text{count of term } t \text{ in document } d" />

<MathBlock tex="\text{IDF}(t) = \log\left(\frac{N}{1 + \text{DF}(t)}\right) \qquad \text{TF-IDF}(t,d) = \text{TF}(t,d) \times \text{IDF}(t)" />

`N` is the total number of documents; `DF(t)` ("document frequency") is how many documents
contain term `t` at least once. A word appearing in every document has `DF(t)=N`, driving
`IDF(t) = \log(N/(N+1))` — a value just *below* zero, the smallest (most suppressed) IDF any
word in the corpus can get. A rare word (small `DF(t)`) gets a large *positive* `IDF(t)`,
amplifying its contribution instead.

<Callout kind="tip" title="The '+1' in the denominator matters">
  Without it, a hypothetical term with `DF(t)=0` (never seen in the training corpus at all)
  would divide by zero. Adding `1` to the denominator avoids that edge case — as a side effect,
  it also means a term appearing in literally *every* document gets `IDF(t) = log(N/(N+1))`,
  a small *negative* number rather than exactly zero (verified directly below). That's a
  harmless quirk of this particular smoothing choice, not a bug: such a term still ends up with
  the lowest IDF of any word in the corpus, which is the actual property TF-IDF needs.
</Callout>

<h2 id="python">TF-IDF, computed and compared directly against plain counts</h2>

<CodeCell
  code={"import numpy as np\nimport re\n\ndef tokenize(text):\n    return [tok for tok in re.split(r'[^a-zA-Z]+', text.lower()) if tok]\n\ncorpus = [\n    \"the cat sat on the mat\",\n    \"the dog sat on the mat\",\n    \"the bird flew over the mat\",\n]\ntokenized = [tokenize(doc) for doc in corpus]\nvocabulary = sorted(set(w for doc in tokenized for w in doc))\nword_to_index = {w: i for i, w in enumerate(vocabulary)}\nN = len(corpus)\n\n# term frequency: plain counts, exactly like bag-of-words\ntf = np.zeros((N, len(vocabulary)))\nfor doc_idx, doc in enumerate(tokenized):\n    for word in doc:\n        tf[doc_idx, word_to_index[word]] += 1\n\n# document frequency: how many DOCUMENTS contain each word at least once\ndf = np.zeros(len(vocabulary))\nfor word, idx in word_to_index.items():\n    df[idx] = sum(1 for doc in tokenized if word in doc)\n\nidf = np.log(N / (1 + df))\ntfidf = tf * idf\n\nprint(\"vocabulary:\", vocabulary)\nprint()\nprint(f\"{'word':>6} {'DF':>4} {'IDF':>8}\")\nfor word, idx in word_to_index.items():\n    print(f\"{word:>6} {int(df[idx]):>4} {idf[idx]:>8.4f}\")\nprint()\n\n# compare 'the' and 'mat' (in EVERY document) against 'cat', 'dog', 'bird' (in only ONE document each)\nfor word in [\"the\", \"mat\", \"cat\", \"dog\", \"bird\"]:\n    idx = word_to_index[word]\n    print(f\"'{word}':  DF={int(df[idx])}/{N}  IDF={idf[idx]:.4f}  TF-IDF in doc 0={tfidf[0, idx]:.4f}\")\n\nprint()\nprint(\"'the' and 'mat' appear in ALL 3 documents -> IDF is NEGATIVE (log(3/4)) -> lowest TF-IDF\")\nprint(\"in the corpus, even though 'the' has a HIGHER raw count (2) than the rarer words (1 each).\")"}
  output={"vocabulary: ['bird', 'cat', 'dog', 'flew', 'mat', 'on', 'over', 'sat', 'the']\n\n  word   DF      IDF\n  bird    1   0.4055\n   cat    1   0.4055\n   dog    1   0.4055\n  flew    1   0.4055\n   mat    3  -0.2877\n    on    2   0.0000\n  over    1   0.4055\n   sat    2   0.0000\n   the    3  -0.2877\n\n'the':  DF=3/3  IDF=-0.2877  TF-IDF in doc 0=-0.5754\n'mat':  DF=3/3  IDF=-0.2877  TF-IDF in doc 0=-0.2877\n'cat':  DF=1/3  IDF=0.4055  TF-IDF in doc 0=0.4055\n'dog':  DF=1/3  IDF=0.4055  TF-IDF in doc 0=0.0000\n'bird':  DF=1/3  IDF=0.4055  TF-IDF in doc 0=0.0000\n\n'the' and 'mat' appear in ALL 3 documents -> IDF is NEGATIVE (log(3/4)) -> lowest TF-IDF\nin the corpus, even though 'the' has a HIGHER raw count (2) than the rarer words (1 each)."}

## Summary

TF-IDF multiplies a word's raw count within a document (TF) by a corpus-wide rarity weight
(IDF), which collapses toward zero for words appearing in nearly every document and grows for
rare, more distinguishing words — verified directly above by comparing "the"/"mat" (in every
document) against "cat"/"dog"/"bird" (each in exactly one). This gives a more useful text
representation than plain bag-of-words counts for many downstream tasks, including the Naive
Bayes classifier built next lesson. The next lesson turns to n-grams — capturing some of the
word-order information both bag-of-words and TF-IDF completely discard.
