<h2 id="intuition">Closing week three: a bad answer has several possible causes</h2>

"The RAG system gave a wrong answer" is not a diagnosis. There are four distinct failure points with four different fixes, and treating them as one problem leads to tuning the wrong stage — usually the prompt, which is rarely the culprit.

<h2 id="math">Four Places It Breaks</h2>

**1. Not in the corpus.** No retrieval strategy recovers a fact that was never indexed. The correct behaviour is abstention, which requires a confidence gate.

**2. In the corpus, not retrieved.** The vocabulary gap from week one. Fixes: better embeddings, hybrid search, query transformation.

**3. Retrieved, but ranked below the cutoff.** Recall is fine and MRR is poor. Fixes: reranking, or a larger `k`.

**4. Retrieved and used, but the answer is still wrong.** The generation stage ignored or misread the context. Fixes: grounding instructions, citation requirements, groundedness checks.

Distinguishing 2 from 3 needs recall@k **and** MRR — which is exactly why week four measures both.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Retrieval quality is the ceiling on everything downstream">
  No prompt engineering rescues a pipeline whose retriever missed the answer. If recall@10 is 0.571 — as measured on paraphrased queries this month — then 43% of questions are unanswerable no matter how good the generation is. Debug the retriever first, always. Time spent tuning prompts against a broken retriever is wasted.
</Callout>

## Week three summary

RAG breaks in four distinct places: the fact is absent from the corpus (abstain), present but not retrieved (better embeddings, hybrid, query transformation), retrieved but ranked too low (rerank, raise `k`), or retrieved and still answered wrongly (grounding, citations). Recall@k and MRR together localize which. Retrieval quality caps everything downstream — at recall@10 of 0.571, 43% of questions are unanswerable regardless of prompting. Week four measures all of it properly.
