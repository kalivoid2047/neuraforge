<h2 id="intuition">Encode position as a rotation, not an addition</h2>

Sinusoidal PE *adds* a position-dependent vector to the embedding before attention. That works,
but the relationship between two positions' encodings isn't directly visible to the dot product
that attention actually computes — the model has to *learn* to extract relative position from
the sum. Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), used in GPT-NeoX, LLaMA, and most modern open LLMs,
takes a different approach: instead of adding a position vector, it **rotates** the query and
key vectors by an angle proportional to their position, applied in pairs of dimensions.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Why rotation specifically">
  A rotation has a property addition doesn't: the dot product of two rotated vectors depends
  only on the *difference* in their rotation angles, not their absolute angles. Rotate query at
  position 5 and key at position 3 by their respective amounts, and their dot product ends up
  depending only on `5 - 3 = 2` — relative position falls out automatically, without the model
  having to learn to compute it.
</Callout>

<h2 id="math">Rotation in 2D, extended to many dimensions</h2>

For a single pair of dimensions `(x, y)`, rotating by angle `θ`:

<MathBlock tex="\begin{pmatrix}x'\\y'\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta & -\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}" />

RoPE applies this to a query or key vector by splitting its `d_k` dimensions into `d_k/2` pairs,
each rotated by an angle that depends on **both** the pair index `i` and the token's position
`pos`:

<MathBlock tex="\theta_{i} = pos \cdot 10000^{-2i/d_k}" />

— the same frequency schedule idea as sinusoidal PE, but used as a rotation angle instead of an
additive term. The key identity that makes this work: rotating query by angle `θ_m` (its
position) and key by angle `θ_n` (its position), then dotting them, is mathematically
equivalent to rotating by `θ_m − θ_n` — the relative offset.

<h2 id="python">Rotating Q/K, then verifying relative-position-only dependence</h2>

<CodeCell

Two completely different absolute position pairs, same relative offset (5), same score — this
is the property that doesn't hold for additive sinusoidal PE, and it's why RoPE-based models
generalize better to sequence lengths not seen during training: the model only ever has to
reason about relative offsets, which it saw plenty of at every length during training.

## Summary

RoPE encodes position as a rotation applied to query and key vectors, split into pairs of
dimensions, rather than as an additive term. The key payoff: the resulting attention score
between two positions depends only on their *relative* offset, not their absolute positions —
verified directly above by matching scores for two token pairs with the same gap but different
absolute positions. This relative-position property is a major reason RoPE-based models
generalize better beyond their training sequence length than sinusoidal-PE models.
