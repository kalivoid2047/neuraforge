<h2 id="intuition">The historical default activation functions</h2>

Sigmoid and tanh were the standard activation functions for the first few decades of neural
network research (largely superseded by ReLU for hidden layers, next lesson — but still
important, and sigmoid remains the standard choice for binary-classification output layers).
Both take any real number and **squash** it into a bounded range, smoothly.

<h2 id="math">Sigmoid and tanh, defined</h2>

<MathBlock tex="\sigma(z) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-z}} \qquad \text{range: } (0, 1)" />
<MathBlock tex="\tanh(z) = \frac{e^{z}-e^{-z}}{e^{z}+e^{-z}} \qquad \text{range: } (-1, 1)" />

They're closely related — tanh is a rescaled, shifted sigmoid:

<MathBlock tex="\tanh(z) = 2\sigma(2z) - 1" />

Sigmoid's `(0, 1)` range makes it a natural choice for outputs meant to represent a probability.
Tanh's `(-1, 1)` range is zero-centered, which in practice tends to help optimization slightly
compared to sigmoid's always-positive output — a detail worth knowing, though the bigger issue
both share is more important:

<Callout kind="danger" title="The vanishing gradient problem">
  Both functions **saturate** at extreme input values — their output flattens out near 0 or 1
  (sigmoid) or −1 or 1 (tanh), meaning their *slope* (gradient) approaches zero. During
  backpropagation (Month 3), a near-zero gradient at any layer multiplies through everything
  before it, shrinking the gradient signal that reaches earlier layers — in deep networks, this
  can make early layers train extremely slowly or not at all. This single issue is a major part
  of why ReLU (next lesson) displaced sigmoid/tanh as the default choice for hidden layers.
</Callout>

## Summary

Sigmoid squashes to `(0, 1)`, tanh to `(−1, 1)` — closely related functions (`tanh(z) =
2σ(2z) − 1`), both historically standard but both prone to saturation: at extreme inputs, their
gradient approaches zero, which compounds through backpropagation's chain rule to slow or stall
training in deep networks (the vanishing gradient problem). This is the main motivation for
ReLU, covered next.
