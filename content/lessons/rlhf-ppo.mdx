<h2 id="intuition">Using the reward model to change the policy</h2>

With a reward model in hand, the obvious move is to train the language model to maximize it. That is **RLHF**: treat the model as a policy, sample responses, score them with the reward model, and push up the probability of high-scoring ones. The complication is that a learned reward model is an *approximation* of human preference, and optimizing hard against an approximation finds its flaws rather than the thing it approximates.

<h2 id="math">Optimizing Against a Learned Reward</h2>

The objective adds a KL penalty against the frozen starting policy:

<MathBlock tex="\max_\pi \; \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi}\big[r(x,y)\big] - \beta\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(\pi \,\|\, \pi_{\text{ref}}\big)" />

The first term chases reward; the second (Month 5's KL divergence) penalizes drifting from the SFT model. Without it, the policy reliably finds degenerate outputs that score highly under the reward model and are worthless to humans — **reward hacking**.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Four models in memory at once">
  PPO requires the **policy** being trained, a frozen **reference** for the KL term, the **reward model**, and a **value head** for advantage estimation. On top of that it needs online sampling — generation inside the training loop — and the KL coefficient is notoriously sensitive. The combination of memory pressure, a slow sampling loop, and delicate hyperparameters is precisely why the next lesson's alternative displaced it for most practical work.
</Callout>

## Summary

RLHF treats the language model as a policy and maximizes a learned reward, subject to a KL penalty against the SFT reference. That penalty is essential rather than decorative: a reward model is a proxy, and unconstrained optimization against a proxy produces reward hacking — outputs that score well and are useless. Operationally PPO is demanding, needing policy, reference, reward model, and value head in memory plus an online sampling loop and a sensitive KL coefficient. The next lesson achieves the same alignment with a supervised loss and none of that machinery.
