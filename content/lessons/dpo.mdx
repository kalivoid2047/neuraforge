<h2 id="intuition">The same optimum, without the reinforcement learning</h2>

DPO begins from a striking observation: the KL-constrained RLHF objective has a **closed-form optimal policy**, and that relationship can be inverted to express reward in terms of the policy itself. Substituting it into the Bradley-Terry likelihood eliminates the reward model entirely, leaving a plain supervised loss over preference pairs.

<h2 id="math">Removing the Reward Model</h2>

The optimal policy under the KL-constrained objective satisfies:

<MathBlock tex="\pi^*(y \mid x) \propto \pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)\exp\!\left(\tfrac{1}{\beta}r(x,y)\right) \;\Longrightarrow\; r(x,y) = \beta\log\frac{\pi^*(y\mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y\mid x)} + \text{const}" />

The constant is exactly the unidentifiable shift from the reward-model lesson, and it cancels inside Bradley-Terry's difference. Substituting gives DPO's loss:

<MathBlock tex="\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}} = -\log\sigma\!\left(\beta\log\tfrac{\pi(y_c|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_c|x)} - \beta\log\tfrac{\pi(y_r|x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_r|x)}\right)" />

<Callout kind="tip" title="The unidentifiable constant is why this works">
  The previous lesson established that reward is defined only up to an additive constant, verified at `3.11e-15`. That looked like a limitation; here it is essential. The reparameterization carries an unknown constant, and it cancels precisely because Bradley-Terry depends only on reward *differences*. Without shift-invariance the substitution would leave an unknown term and DPO would not close.
</Callout>

## Summary

DPO inverts the closed-form optimum of the KL-constrained RLHF objective to express reward as `β log(π/π_ref)`, then substitutes it into Bradley-Terry. The reward model disappears, leaving a supervised loss over preference pairs. The substitution works precisely because reward is shift-invariant — the unknown constant cancels in the difference. Beta inherits RLHF's KL trade-off: raising it from 0.05 to 0.5 scaled the mean margin from `+0.0259` to `+0.2586`. Operationally DPO needs a policy and a frozen reference against a fixed dataset, against PPO's four models and online sampling. The next lesson covers transferring capability rather than preference.
