<h2 id="intuition">The three knobs that actually matter</h2>

LoRA has few hyperparameters, and only three carry most of the outcome: the **rank** `r`, the scaling factor **alpha**, and **which modules** get adapters. Each has a clear role, and the common mistakes with each are predictable.

<h2 id="math">Three Hyperparameters</h2>

**Rank `r`** sets adapter capacity. It costs `2dr` parameters per adapted matrix, so cost is linear in `r` while capacity saturates — the SVD table in the previous lesson showed exactly this, with everything above the update's true rank adding parameters and no accuracy.

**Alpha** scales the adapter's contribution:

<MathBlock tex="\Delta W = \frac{\alpha}{r}BA" />

Dividing by `r` is what makes alpha meaningful independently of rank: **doubling `r` does not double the update's magnitude**, so you can search `r` without re-tuning the effective learning rate. Conventional practice sets `α = 2r` or `α = r`.

**Target modules** decide where adaptation can happen at all. The original paper adapted only `W_q` and `W_v`; later practice often adapts all four attention projections and sometimes the MLP.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Raising r is usually the wrong first response to underfitting">
  If a LoRA fine-tune underfits, the instinct is to raise `r`. That is usually not the binding constraint — the previous lesson's SVD table showed accuracy saturating sharply once `r` reaches the update's intrinsic rank, with higher ranks contributing nothing. **Adapting more modules** typically helps more than raising rank on the modules you already have, because it increases where the model can change rather than how much it can change in one place. Check the learning rate and target modules before reaching for `r`.
</Callout>

## Summary

Rank `r` sets adapter capacity at a cost of `2dr` per matrix, with accuracy saturating once `r` reaches the update's intrinsic rank. Alpha scales the contribution, and the `α/r` form is what decouples strength from capacity so rank can be searched without retuning the effective learning rate. Target modules determine where the model is permitted to change at all, and widening them is usually a better response to underfitting than raising rank. The next lesson reduces the frozen weights themselves.
