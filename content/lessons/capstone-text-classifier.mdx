<h2 id="intuition">The month's arc, in one pipeline</h2>

Trace the path from the first lesson to here: probability axioms → conditional probability →
Bayes' theorem → random variables and distributions → entropy → cross-entropy → KL divergence
→ softmax derived from first principles → maximum likelihood estimation → text preprocessing →
bag-of-words → TF-IDF → n-grams → Naive Bayes (Bayes' theorem, applied) → precision/recall/F1
→ cross-validation → a neural classifier (Month 4's tools, applied to text) → bias-variance
trade-off. This capstone's single pipeline uses nearly all of it: real text, properly
preprocessed and vectorized, classified by two genuinely different approaches, evaluated
properly rather than by accuracy alone.

<h2 id="math">The full pipeline</h2>

<MathBlock tex="\text{raw text} \xrightarrow{\text{preprocess}} \text{tokens} \xrightarrow{\text{TF-IDF}} \text{vector} \xrightarrow{\text{classifier}} \hat{y} \xrightarrow{\text{precision/recall/F1, via CV}} \text{honest evaluation}" />

Two classifiers, trained on the *same* preprocessed, vectorized data, evaluated the *same* way:
**Naive Bayes** (explicit probabilistic model, Bayes' theorem plus the independence assumption)
and a **small neural network** (Month 4's `Linear → ReLU → Linear → softmax`, trained with
cross-entropy and Adam) — a direct, fair comparison of two different modeling philosophies on
identical data.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Both classifiers, evaluated fairly, on the same data">
  Naive Bayes and the neural classifier see the same preprocessed, split data and are scored by
  the same precision/recall/F1 metrics, averaged over the same 5 cross-validation folds — a fair
  comparison, not a cherry-picked single split. Neither approach is "correct" in general; this is
  exactly the kind of evidence-based comparison week four's methodology exists to produce.
</Callout>

## Month 5 summary

This month built the mathematical foundation for reasoning under uncertainty and applied it
directly to text: probability axioms, conditional probability, and Bayes' theorem (week one);
entropy, cross-entropy, KL divergence, and softmax, all connected through maximum likelihood
estimation to explain *why* Month 4's loss function has the form it does (week two); the
classical NLP pipeline — preprocessing, bag-of-words, TF-IDF, n-grams, and Naive Bayes — built
entirely from that same probability foundation (week three); and proper evaluation methodology —
precision/recall/F1, cross-validation, and the bias-variance trade-off — culminating in this
capstone's side-by-side comparison of an explicit probabilistic classifier against a neural one,
evaluated fairly on identical data. Month 6 turns to embeddings and language modeling, building
the representations that let words be compared by meaning rather than exact string match.
