<h2 id="intuition">Don't change the weights — learn a small correction to them</h2>

Week one's dilemma was that full fine-tuning adapts well but damages the model, while freezing
preserves the model but adapts poorly. **LoRA** refuses the trade with a specific observation:
the *update* fine-tuning learns is far simpler than the weights it modifies.

A weight matrix is full-rank — it has to be, to encode everything pretraining learned. But the
**change** needed to adapt it to one narrow task is not. LoRA freezes `W₀` entirely and learns
only a low-rank correction alongside it.

<h2 id="math">Factorizing the update</h2>

<MathBlock tex="W = W_0 + \Delta W \qquad \Delta W = \frac{\alpha}{r} BA \quad\text{where}\quad B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r},\; A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}" />

`W₀` never receives a gradient. Only `A` and `B` train, and since `r ≪ d`, they hold a tiny
fraction of the parameters — `2dr` instead of `d²`.

The forward pass adds a branch rather than modifying the original:

<MathBlock tex="h = xW_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r}(xA^\top)B^\top" />

<Callout kind="tip" title="B starts at exactly zero — and that matters">
  `A` is randomly initialized but `B` is **zero**, so `BA = 0` and the adapter contributes
  literally nothing at step 0. The model begins fine-tuning behaving *identically* to the
  pretrained model, then departs from it gradually as `B` learns. Initializing both randomly would
  inject noise into a carefully-trained model before training even starts. The check below
  confirms the difference is exactly `0.000e+00`, not merely small.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning" title="This experiment assumes the hypothesis rather than proving it">
  The update above was *constructed* to be rank 8, so recovering it perfectly at `r=8` is
  arithmetic, not evidence. What the cell genuinely demonstrates is the **consequence**: *if* an
  update has low rank, a rank-`r` factorization captures it exactly at a small fraction of the
  parameters, and the SVD table shows precisely how much is lost at each `r` when it does not.

  Whether real fine-tuning updates are approximately low-rank is an **empirical** claim, and the
  evidence for it is that LoRA works — across many models and tasks it matches full fine-tuning
  closely. That is strong practical support, not a proof, and it is why `r` remains a
  hyperparameter you tune rather than a value you derive.
</Callout>

## Summary

LoRA freezes the pretrained matrix and learns a low-rank correction `ΔW = (α/r)BA`, training
`2dr` parameters instead of `d²`. The SVD table quantifies the trade: a rank-8 factorization of a
genuinely rank-8 update reconstructs it to `1.4e-15` while storing 6.2% as many numbers, and
lower ranks lose measurable energy. `B` initializes to zero so the adapter is exactly a no-op at
step 0 — verified at `0.000e+00` — letting fine-tuning depart gradually from the pretrained
model. For a 7B model at `r=8` that is 4.19M trainable parameters, **0.0626%** of the whole. The
next lesson covers choosing `r`, `α`, and which modules to target.
