<h2 id="intuition">Week three: deciding what to do before doing it</h2>

Week one's loop decided one step at a time. That is **ReAct**, and it is not the only option. **Plan-then-execute** commits to a whole plan up front and then carries it out. The two differ in what they can optimize and, more usefully, in how they fail.

<h2 id="math">Decide Now or Decide Once</h2>

**ReAct** — the policy sees every observation before choosing the next action. Maximally adaptive, strictly sequential, one model call per step.

**Plan-then-execute** — one call produces the whole plan; execution follows it. The plan can be analyzed, validated, and parallelized before anything runs, but it was written before any result came back.

Their failure modes are opposites: ReAct **wanders** (each local step is reasonable, the trajectory is not); planning **executes a stale plan** (step 2 invalidated the plan and nothing noticed).

<Callout kind="tip" title="Most production agents are hybrids">
  The usual arrangement is to plan, execute a few steps, then **replan** — either on a fixed cadence or when an observation contradicts an assumption. That recovers most of planning's concurrency while keeping most of ReAct's adaptivity, at the cost of extra planning calls. The interesting parameter is the replan trigger, and it is almost always tuned empirically rather than derived.
</Callout>

## Summary

ReAct decides one step at a time and adapts immediately, at the cost of strict sequencing; plan-then-execute decides once, enabling validation and concurrency, at the cost of acting on pre-result assumptions. They fail in opposite ways — wandering versus executing a stale plan — which is why production agents usually plan, execute a few steps, and replan. The next lesson shows what a plan makes possible.
