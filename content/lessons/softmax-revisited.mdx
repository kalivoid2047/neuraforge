<h2 id="intuition">Softmax wasn't an arbitrary formula choice</h2>

Month 4 introduced softmax as "the function that converts logits into probabilities" without
asking *why that specific formula*, rather than some other way of normalizing numbers into
`[0,1]` summing to `1`. This lesson derives it from a natural requirement, and shows that
training with cross-entropy against a one-hot label is *exactly* minimizing KL divergence to the
true distribution — connecting every information-theoretic idea this week into one coherent
picture.

<h2 id="math">Deriving softmax</h2>

Suppose a network's raw logits `z_i` are meant to represent *relative log-probabilities* — a
natural requirement being that the *ratio* between any two classes' probabilities,
`Q(i)/Q(j)`, should depend only on the *difference* `z_i - z_j`, not on their absolute values or
on any other logit (shifting every logit by the same constant, or changing an unrelated class's
logit, shouldn't affect how two OTHER classes compare to each other). That requirement forces
`Q(i) \propto e^{z_i}`; normalizing so probabilities sum to `1` gives exactly softmax:

<MathBlock tex="Q(i) = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}" />

There is no other normalization that satisfies that "probability ratio depends only on the
logit difference" requirement — softmax is the *unique* answer, not one arbitrary option among
many.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Training with cross-entropy IS minimizing KL divergence">
  For a one-hot true label `P`, `H(P)=0` (zero entropy — no uncertainty, verified in the entropy
  lesson). Since `D_{KL}(P\|Q) = H(P,Q) - H(P) = H(P,Q) - 0 = H(P,Q)`, minimizing cross-entropy
  loss and minimizing KL divergence to the true label are *exactly the same optimization
  problem* whenever the true label is one-hot. Training a classifier with cross-entropy loss
  literally is training it to minimize KL divergence to the true distribution.
</Callout>

## Summary

Softmax, `Q(i) = e^{z_i}/Σe^{z_j}`, is the unique normalization satisfying a natural
requirement: the ratio between any two classes' probabilities should depend only on the
difference between their logits — verified directly above. Because a one-hot true label has zero
entropy, minimizing cross-entropy against it is *exactly* minimizing KL divergence to the true
distribution, not merely a related idea. This closes the information-theoretic story behind
Month 4's loss function. The final lesson this week connects all of this to maximum likelihood
estimation — the general statistical principle that cross-entropy minimization turns out to be
a special case of.
