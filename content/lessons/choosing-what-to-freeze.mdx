<h2 id="intuition">Closing week one: how much of the model should be allowed to move?</h2>

The two experiments so far are the endpoints of a spectrum. Full fine-tuning lets every parameter move: maximum adaptation, maximum damage. A frozen backbone lets almost none move: minimal damage, reduced adaptation. Neither is obviously right, and the real question is where on that spectrum a given problem belongs.

<h2 id="math">A Spectrum, Not a Binary</h2>

The trade is visible in the measurements directly:

- **Full fine-tune** — task B `1.000`, task A representation recovery `0.912`, all 316 parameters trainable.
- **Frozen backbone** — task B `0.873`, task A representation recovery `1.000`, 100 parameters trainable (31.6%).

Full fine-tuning bought 0.127 more accuracy on the new task and paid for it with degraded features and 3.2x the trainable parameters.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Which layers to freeze, if you are freezing by depth">
  The conventional ordering is that early layers learn general features and later layers learn task-specific ones, so freezing *early* layers and training *late* ones preserves the most reusable structure. **Gradual unfreezing** refines this further: train only the head first, then progressively unfreeze deeper layers as training stabilizes. Both are heuristics rather than laws — for a sufficiently different target task, early-layer features may genuinely need to move.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning" title="Neither endpoint is satisfying — which is the point">
  Full fine-tuning gives the best new-task accuracy and damages the model. Freezing preserves the
  model and gives up accuracy. The measured gap here is `1.000` versus `0.873` on task B.

  Week two's answer is to refuse the trade: **add a small number of new trainable parameters**
  while leaving every pretrained weight frozen. That gets close to full fine-tuning's adaptation
  with the frozen backbone's preservation, at a fraction of the memory — which is why LoRA
  displaced both of these approaches for most practical work.
</Callout>

## Summary

Full fine-tuning and a frozen backbone are endpoints of one spectrum: measured here, full fine-tuning reached `1.000` on the new task with `0.912` feature recovery, while freezing reached `0.873` with `1.000` recovery using 31.6% of the trainable parameters. Freezing early layers and training late ones, or unfreezing gradually, moves along that spectrum but does not escape it. Week two escapes it — by adding a few new trainable parameters instead of choosing which old ones to sacrifice.
