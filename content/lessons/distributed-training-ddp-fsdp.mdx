<h2 id="intuition">Closing week two: when one GPU is not enough</h2>

Packing, mixed precision, accumulation, and checkpointing all squeeze more out of a *single*
device. Past a certain model size none of it is enough, and the work has to spread across many
GPUs. There are two fundamentally different ways to do that, and the difference decides whether
adding hardware lets you train a *bigger* model or merely a *faster* one.

<h2 id="math">Replicate or shard</h2>

**DDP (Distributed Data Parallel)** — every GPU holds a complete copy of the model and optimizer
state, processes a different slice of the batch, and the gradients are **all-reduced** (averaged
across devices) before each step. Since every device stores everything:

<MathBlock tex="\text{memory}_{\text{DDP}} = 16 \times N_{\text{params}} \quad \text{per GPU, independent of GPU count}" />

**FSDP (Fully Sharded Data Parallel)** — weights, gradients, and optimizer state are *split*
across devices. Each GPU stores only its shard, and gathers a layer's full weights transiently
when that layer runs:

<MathBlock tex="\text{memory}_{\text{FSDP}} \approx \frac{16 \times N_{\text{params}}}{N_{\text{GPUs}}} \quad \text{per GPU}" />

<Callout kind="tip" title="These compose with everything from this week">
  DDP and FSDP are orthogonal to the single-device techniques. A real frontier training run uses
  all of them at once: packed data, bf16 mixed precision, gradient accumulation for a large
  effective batch, gradient checkpointing to fit activations, and FSDP to shard state across
  thousands of GPUs. Each addresses a different bottleneck.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning" title="A 7B model cannot be trained with plain DDP on 80GB GPUs — at any cluster size">
  Read the two tables together. A 7B model needs **112 GB** per GPU under DDP, and the DDP
  ceiling on an 80 GB card is **5.0B parameters** — a number that does not move whether you have
  1 GPU or 512. Adding hardware under DDP buys throughput and nothing else.

  This is the single most important practical consequence of the replicate-versus-shard choice.
  "Just add more GPUs" solves a speed problem; it does not solve a capacity problem. Only
  sharding raises the ceiling.
</Callout>

## Week two summary

Training at scale is five separate problems with five separate fixes: packing eliminates padding
waste (45.7% recovered), mixed precision buys tensor-core throughput while — contrary to common
belief — saving no parameter memory, gradient accumulation reaches large effective batches with
numerically identical updates, checkpointing cuts stored activations from `O(n)` to `O(√n)` for
33% more compute, and FSDP shards state so the trainable-model ceiling grows with the cluster
instead of staying fixed at DDP's 5.0B. Real runs use all five together. Week three turns from
training to inference.
