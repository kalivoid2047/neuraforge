<h2 id="intuition">The single largest win in LLM serving</h2>

A GPU running a batch of one is mostly idle: the fixed cost of a forward pass is paid whether one sequence or sixty-four go through it. Batching amortizes that fixed cost — and makes every request in the batch wait for the whole batch.

<h2 id="math">One Dial, Two Directions</h2>

Model one forward pass as a fixed cost plus a marginal cost per sequence:

<MathBlock tex="t(B) = t_{\text{fixed}} + t_{\text{marginal}}\,B \qquad \text{throughput} = \frac{B}{t(B)}" />

Throughput rises with `B` and saturates; per-request latency rises with `B` and does not. There is no `B` that optimizes both, which is why batch size is a **dial set from your SLO** rather than a value to be tuned to an optimum.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Continuous batching recovered 51.3% of the slots">
  **Static** batching holds every slot until the *longest* sequence in the batch finishes. On the measured mix of output lengths, a 12-token reply occupied its slot for all 420 steps, and **51.3%** of all slot-steps were wasted holding finished sequences. **Continuous** batching evicts completed sequences each step and admits new ones, recovering that as real capacity. This is the largest single win in LLM serving and the reason vLLM and TGI exist rather than a loop around `model.generate()`.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="warning" title="A model, not a benchmark">
  There is no GPU or web server in this browser, so the numbers below come from an explicit
  cost *model* — stated constants for service time, throughput and price — not from a
  measurement of real hardware. That is the honest framing, and it is also how capacity planning
  is actually done before the hardware is bought. What the cell demonstrates is the **shape** of
  the trade: which way each curve bends, and where it goes vertical. Substitute your own measured
  constants and the conclusions follow the same shape.
</Callout>

## Summary

Batching amortizes a forward pass's fixed cost, raising throughput `8.8×` from batch 1 to 64 while raising per-request latency in step — so batch size is a dial set from the SLO, not an optimum. Static batching holds every slot until the longest sequence finishes, wasting a measured **51.3%** of slot-steps on realistic output-length variance; continuous batching reclaims it. The next lesson covers what bounds the batch.
