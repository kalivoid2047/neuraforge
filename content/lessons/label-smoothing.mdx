<h2 id="intuition">Two small training tricks with outsized effects</h2>

Neither of these changes the model's architecture at all — they change *what target the model
is pushed toward* (label smoothing) and *how the learning rate moves over time* (schedules).
Both were found empirically to matter a lot for Transformer training quality, and both are used
in essentially every serious Transformer training run.

<h2 id="math">Label smoothing</h2>

Standard cross-entropy trains the model to push the target class's probability toward exactly
1.0 and everything else toward exactly 0.0 — the model is rewarded for arbitrarily extreme
confidence, which encourages overfitting and makes the model poorly calibrated (confidently
wrong, not just wrong). Label smoothing replaces the one-hot target with a slightly softened
distribution:

<MathBlock tex="y^{\text{smooth}}_i = (1-\epsilon)\cdot y_i + \frac{\epsilon}{K}" />

where `K` is the vocabulary size and `ε` (typically 0.1) is the smoothing amount. The correct
class still gets the highest target probability, but it's capped below 1.0, and every incorrect
class gets a small nonzero target instead of exactly 0.

## Summary

Label smoothing softens the training target away from a one-hot distribution, discouraging
overconfident predictions and improving calibration — implemented as a small reduction in the
target class's probability, redistributed uniformly to all classes. Learning rate warmup (small
early on, rising to a peak, then decaying) stabilizes early training when weight and optimizer
statistics are least reliable — both are cheap, architecture-independent changes with
consistently positive empirical effects on Transformer training.
