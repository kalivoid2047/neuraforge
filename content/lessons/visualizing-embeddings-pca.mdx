<h2 id="intuition">Seeing a 300-dimensional space on a flat screen</h2>

Embeddings live in hundreds of dimensions; humans see two. **Principal Component Analysis
(PCA)** finds the directions along which the data varies *most*, then projects onto the top few
— keeping as much of the original spread as possible in the reduced space. This directly reuses
Month 2's eigenvalues and eigenvectors: PCA is precisely the eigendecomposition of the data's
covariance matrix.

<h2 id="math">PCA via eigendecomposition</h2>

Given centered data `X` (mean subtracted from every column):

<MathBlock tex="\Sigma = \frac{1}{n-1}X^\top X \qquad \Sigma v_i = \lambda_i v_i" />

The eigenvectors `v_i` of the covariance matrix are the **principal components** — the
directions of greatest variance — and each eigenvalue `λ_i` is exactly how much variance lies
along its direction. Sorting by eigenvalue and keeping the top `k` gives the best possible
`k`-dimensional linear projection, in the sense of preserving the most variance.

The **explained variance ratio** for the top `k` components says how much of the total spread
survived the projection:

<MathBlock tex="\text{explained variance ratio} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_i}{\sum_{j}\lambda_j}" />

<Callout kind="warning" title="A 2D embedding plot can be actively misleading">
  If the top two components explain only, say, 25% of the variance, then 75% of the structure
  that actually distinguishes those embeddings is *invisible* in the plot. Words that appear
  adjacent on screen may be far apart in the real space, and vice versa. Always report the
  explained variance ratio alongside any embedding visualization — a beautiful-looking cluster
  plot with 15% explained variance is close to decorative.
</Callout>

## Summary

PCA projects high-dimensional embeddings into a viewable 2D space by finding the eigenvectors of
the covariance matrix — directly reusing Month 2's eigendecomposition — and keeping those with
the largest eigenvalues, since each eigenvalue *is* the variance along its direction. The
explained variance ratio quantifies how much real structure survived the projection, and must be
reported alongside any embedding plot: a low value means most of what distinguishes the
embeddings is invisible on screen. Verified above with three semantic groups whose separation
survives the projection cleanly. The next lesson connects embeddings back to language modeling
before the capstone.
