<h2 id="intuition">The transcript is resent on every single step</h2>

Agent cost is routinely underestimated because the wrong quantity gets measured. The transcript grows linearly and looks manageable. The bill does not follow the transcript.

<h2 id="math">Linear Transcript, Quadratic Work</h2>

At step `n` the prompt contains the system message, the tool schemas, and every prior action and observation. So the **final** transcript is:

<MathBlock tex="T(n) = S + nP \qquad \text{(linear)}" />

but the total tokens the model actually processes across the run is the sum of every intermediate prompt:

<MathBlock tex="\sum_{i=1}^{n} (S + iP) = nS + P\frac{n(n+1)}{2} \;=\; O(n^2)" />

Doubling the step budget roughly **quadruples** the cost.

<Callout kind="warning" title="This is also why long agents degrade">
  The quadratic is a billing problem, but the linear part is a quality problem. By step 50 the transcript holds 49 prior observations, most of them irrelevant to the current decision — and Month 10 measured that models use information in the middle of a long context less reliably than at either end. A long-running agent is simultaneously paying more per step and reasoning over a worse prompt. Week two's memory management addresses both at once.
</Callout>

## Summary

The transcript grows as `S + nP`, but total tokens processed is the sum of every intermediate prompt — `O(n²)`. Measured, a 50-step run ends at 6,400 tokens having sent 173,000: a **27×** gap. Doubling the step budget roughly quadruples the cost. And the same accumulation degrades quality, because most of a long transcript is irrelevant to the current decision. Step budgets are a cost control before they are a safety one.
