<h2 id="intuition">The last capstone: everything, against a budget</h2>

This capstone takes a written requirement and works it through to a deployment plan: capacity from Month 8's KV-cache formula, concurrency from Month 9's quantization, latency from this month's queueing, cost, rollout, an incident, and drift. Two of the six stated requirements do not survive the arithmetic — which is the point, and is much cheaper to discover here than after the hardware is bought.

<h2 id="math">Seven Steps, and Two Requirements That Do Not Survive Them</h2>

The chain, each step feeding the next:

<MathBlock tex="\text{SLO} \to \text{KV capacity} \to \text{slots} \to \text{queueing} \to \text{fleet} \to \text{cost} \to \text{rollout} \to \text{incident} \to \text{drift}" />

Queueing uses **Erlang C** for an `M/M/c` system rather than week one's single-server formula, because the service has `c` concurrent slots and the single-server formula overstates the wait several-fold — visible in the `c=1` row of the table.

<Callout kind="danger" title="Two requirements fail, and both fail before any hardware is bought">
  **The latency SLO is infeasible at any fleet size.** Generating 220 tokens at 32 tok/s takes 6.88s of pure service time, so a 3,000 ms p95 cannot be met by adding machines — only by changing what is measured. Streaming makes *time-to-first-token* the metric, which is both achievable and closer to what users perceive.

**The budget is off by 20×.** 25 rps needs 31 int4 cards at $17,856/month against a $900 budget; inverting the question, $900 buys **one** card sustaining **0.81 rps**. And at 0.81 rps a canary cannot detect anything, so the release strategy has to change too.
</Callout>

## Twelve months

**This month:** the serving stack, queueing (`W = S/(1-ρ)` doubling latency between 80% and 90% utilization), batching (continuous recovers **51.3%** of slot-steps), KV-bounded concurrency, systemd hardening (**12/13**, the exception documented), nginx at the edge, atomic symlink deploys, RED metrics, the tail (`1-(1-p)^N` giving **63.4%** slow pages), error budgets (43.2 minutes at 99.9%), RPO/RTO, unit cost spanning **36×**, canary power, and drift.

**The capstone's honest conclusion:** two of six stated requirements were infeasible, and the arithmetic found both before anything was provisioned. The latency SLO could not be met at any price — only by measuring time-to-first-token instead. The budget supported **0.81 rps** against a stated 25.

**And the through-line of the whole course.** At nearly every stage the honest measurement was less flattering than the intuition: post-LN amplified gradients where the prose said it attenuated; paraphrased queries dropped retrieval to `0.571`; a weak critic cost 39% more calls for nothing; caching left p99 untouched; a ten-minute canary cannot see a doubled error rate. None of that is cause for pessimism — it is the entire difference between a system you have measured and one you merely hope about. Build things, measure them, and report what you actually found.
