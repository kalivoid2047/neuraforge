<h2 id="intuition">Week two: the engineering that makes training feasible</h2>

The architecture is settled; now it has to train on billions of tokens without wasting money.
Week two covers the five pieces that decide whether that is affordable: how data reaches the
GPU (this lesson), numeric precision, gradient accumulation, gradient checkpointing, and
multi-GPU distribution.

Start with the data. Real documents have wildly varying lengths, but a GPU wants uniform
`(batch, context_len)` tensors. The naive fix — pad every document to the context length — means
a 12-token document occupies a 128-token row and the model burns 116 tokens' worth of compute on
padding it will then mask out. At scale that is a very large amount of money spent on nothing.

<h2 id="math">Padding vs packing</h2>

**Padding**: one document per row, filled to `context_len` with a pad token. Utilization is the
mean document length over the context length — poor whenever documents are short or
length-variable.

**Packing**: concatenate the entire corpus into one long token stream (with a separator between
documents), then slice it into consecutive `context_len` chunks:

<MathBlock tex="\text{stream} = [d_1, \text{sep}, d_2, \text{sep}, \ldots] \quad\longrightarrow\quad \text{rows of exactly } \texttt{context\_len}" />

Every slot carries a real token. Utilization is effectively 100%, minus one partial chunk at the
very end.

<Callout kind="tip" title="What to do about cross-document attention">
  A packed row can contain the tail of one document and the head of another, so a position may
  attend backward into text it has no business seeing. Three responses are used in practice:
  (1) **ignore it** — with a separator token and long documents the contamination is small, and
  this is what many large training runs do; (2) **document-aware masking** — extend the causal
  mask so attention additionally cannot cross a separator, which is exact but costs a custom
  mask per row; (3) **best-fit packing** — bin documents so rows rarely straddle boundaries.
  The 45.7%-versus-0% waste measured above is why nobody simply pads instead.
</Callout>

## Summary

Padding each document to the context length wasted 45.7% of token slots on the corpus measured
above; packing — concatenating the corpus into one stream and slicing fixed-length chunks —
wasted none, delivering 1.84× the useful tokens per slot. The cost is that a packed row can span
document boundaries, addressed by separator tokens, document-aware masking, or best-fit binning
depending on how much exactness is worth. The next lesson turns to numeric precision, where a
similar "roughly half the cost for nearly the same result" trade appears.
