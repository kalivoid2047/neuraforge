<h2 id="intuition">Build it, then find out which parts earn their place</h2>

This capstone puts the month together: tools discovered at runtime over MCP, a validated plan, a loop that reads and re-queries, a critic, an allowlist, budgets, and taint tracking — answering questions about **this curriculum**, using the same 18 lessons Month 10 indexed. Then it takes each component away to see what it was worth.

<h2 id="math">The Whole Month, Ablated</h2>

The full pipeline, every stage from this month:

<MathBlock tex="\text{tools/list} \to \text{plan (validated)} \to \text{search} \to \text{read} \to \text{re-query} \to \text{critic} \to \text{guard} \to \text{answer or abstain}" />

The evaluation set is **paraphrased**, not title-echoing — Month 10 measured what happens otherwise: every metric returned `1.000` and meant nothing.

<Callout kind="danger" title="Three findings from the ablation, one of them unwelcome">
  **Reading the top 3 instead of trusting rank 1 is a real win** — it converts a wrong answer into a correct one, because the top-scoring lesson had lower term overlap than the right one.

**Pseudo-relevance feedback only pays once the critic stops discarding its gains** — worth nothing at the strict threshold, worth `+1` at the relaxed one. A single-factor ablation would have called it useless.

**And turning the critic off entirely scores the same 3/4 as the best configured version.** On this evaluation set the critic's net contribution to accuracy is zero: at its strict setting it destroyed two correct answers, and at its best setting it merely stopped doing so.
</Callout>

## Month 11 summary

**Week one** built the loop: a policy, tools the model can only *name*, and a dispatcher that validates every call and returns failures as text so the agent can self-correct. It also priced it — `O(n²)` tokens, a **27×** gap at 50 steps — and stated the governing arithmetic, `p^n`, under which a 95% step completes a 20-step task **35.8%** of the time. **Week two** managed what the agent knows: a recency-only summarizer retained **1 of 5** facts against truncation's **2 of 5**, failed episodes proved the most valuable memories, and MCP turned `N×M` integrations into `N+M` with runtime discovery. **Week three** attacked the exponent: an 8-task plan collapsed to **4 layers**, a strong critic lifted an 8-step task from `0.275` to `0.852`, and a weak one bought nothing for ~39% more calls. **Week four** made it safe, blocking a live injection on **provenance** rather than content.

And the capstone's honest conclusion: the agent's accuracy is bounded by its retriever, which this month did not improve. What the architecture buys is **control** — bounded cost, refused side effects, abstention instead of guessing. Those are operability properties, not accuracy ones, and Month 12 is where operability decides whether the system survives real traffic.
