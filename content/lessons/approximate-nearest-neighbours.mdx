<h2 id="intuition">Closing week one: give up a little recall, gain a lot of speed</h2>

Approximate nearest neighbour search accepts that you will occasionally miss a true neighbour in exchange for scanning a small fraction of the database. The measurement below builds an IVF index — cluster the vectors once, then search only the nearest clusters — and reveals something about ANN that is easy to state and easy to forget.

<h2 id="math">Search Fewer Candidates</h2>

Partition the `n` vectors into `nlist` clusters. At query time, compare against the `nlist` centroids, pick the `nprobe` nearest, and scan only those buckets:

<MathBlock tex="\text{scanned} \approx \text{nlist} + \text{nprobe}\cdot\frac{n}{\text{nlist}} \;\ll\; n" />

`nprobe` is a pure dial: at `nprobe = 1` you scan one bucket, at `nprobe = nlist` you have reconstructed exact search.

<Callout kind="warning" title="ANN speed is a property of your embeddings, not just the algorithm">
  The first version of this experiment used random Gaussian vectors and got **0.110 recall** at `nprobe=1`. Not a bug — random high-dimensional points are nearly equidistant, so there are no clusters for the index to exploit. Rerunning it unchanged on vectors with genuine topical structure — which is what real embeddings have — lifted it to **0.231**, roughly double, purely from the data. Never trust an ANN benchmark run on synthetic vectors; measure on your own.
</Callout>

## Week one summary

Week one built retrieval from the ground up: knowledge in an external store rather than in weights, documents as TF-IDF vectors, cosine similarity chosen because raw dot products can be inflated `3.00×` by padding while cosine moves `0.00e+00`, and exact search costing `O(n)` per query — a flat ~38 µs per 1,000 vectors, extrapolating to an unusable per-query cost at a billion. IVF breaks that linear cost by scanning 1.03% of the database for `0.231` recall, or 25.1% for `0.835`, with `nprobe` as the dial. And that whole trade depends on the embeddings being clustered: on random vectors the same index managed `0.110`. Week two turns to preparing the documents themselves.
