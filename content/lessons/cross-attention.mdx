<h2 id="intuition">Same formula, different source for K and V</h2>

Every attention mechanism you've built so far — including the masked self-attention inside a
decoder block — has one thing in common: Q, K, and V are all projections of the *same*
sequence. Cross-attention breaks that symmetry in exactly one way: **Q comes from one sequence
(the decoder), K and V come from a different sequence (the encoder's output)**. Nothing else
about the formula changes.

<Callout kind="tip" title="The one-line distinction">
  Self-attention: "how should this sequence attend to itself?"
  Cross-attention: "how should sequence A attend to sequence B?" — A's tokens ask questions
  (queries), B's tokens hold the answers (keys and values).
</Callout>

<h2 id="math">Explicit source of every term</h2>

<MathBlock tex="Q = X_{\text{decoder}}W^{Q}, \qquad K = X_{\text{encoder}}W^{K}, \qquad V = X_{\text{encoder}}W^{V}" />
<MathBlock tex="\mathrm{CrossAttention} = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V" />

Note the resulting weight matrix has shape `(tgt_len, src_len)` — **not square**, unlike
self-attention's `(seq_len, seq_len)`. Row `i` (a decoder position) sums to 1 across all
`src_len` columns (encoder positions) — "given decoder position `i`, how should attention be
distributed over the source sequence."

<h2 id="python">Computing and interpreting a cross-attention matrix</h2>

With a trained model on real translation data, you'd see this matrix sharpen dramatically —
"cat" would put most of its weight on "chat," "black" mostly on "noir," roughly tracing the
word-alignment a human translator would draw by hand. With random weights it's close to
uniform, same as every other untrained-attention example in this course — the *shape* and
*structure* of the computation is what matters here, not the specific numbers.

## Summary

Cross-attention uses the exact same scaled dot-product formula as self-attention, with one
structural change: queries come from one sequence (typically the decoder) while keys and values
come from a different sequence (the encoder's output). This makes the resulting weight matrix
rectangular — `(target_len, source_len)` — and lets two sequences of different lengths interact,
which is what actually connects an encoder's understanding of the source to a decoder
generating the target.
