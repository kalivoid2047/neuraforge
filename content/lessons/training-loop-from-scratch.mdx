<h2 id="intuition">Every piece from this month, in five lines</h2>

Every single concept from this month's four weeks comes together in one short loop, repeated
many times:

1. **Forward pass** — run the network on inputs, get predictions (week 3's `MLP`)
2. **Compute loss** — compare predictions to targets with MSE (last week)
3. **Zero gradients** — reset every parameter's `.grad` to `0` (week 3, gradient accumulation)
4. **Backward pass** — call `.backward()` on the loss, computing every weight's gradient at once
   (week 3, reverse-mode autodiff)
5. **Update weights** — subtract a learning-rate-scaled gradient from every weight (this week's
   gradient descent)

This five-step loop, run hundreds or thousands of times, *is* how every neural network you will
ever train gets trained — from the tiny MLP built here to a full production language model. The
scale changes; this loop does not.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Every line traces back to a specific earlier lesson">
  `mlp([Value(xi)...])` is the computational graph (week 2) built automatically by `Value`'s
  operator overloads (week 3). `loss = sum((yp-yt)**2...)` is MSE (last lesson). Zeroing
  `.grad` before `backward()` is the accumulation-bug fix (week 3). `loss.backward()` is
  reverse-mode autodiff (week 2 and 3) walking the topologically-sorted graph. And
  `p.data -= learning_rate * p.grad` is the gradient descent update rule (this week) — applied
  simultaneously to all 21 parameters, because `backward()` already computed every one of their
  gradients in that single call.
</Callout>

## Summary

The five-step training loop — forward pass, compute loss, zero gradients, backward pass, update
weights — assembles every concept from this month into the exact algorithm used to train real
neural networks at any scale. Watching the loss decrease over epochs is watching gradient
descent, powered by reverse-mode autodiff, actually work. The final lesson this month is the
capstone: a clean, complete write-up of the whole engine as a reference implementation.
